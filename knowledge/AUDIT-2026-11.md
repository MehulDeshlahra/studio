## 3. Outside research (20 Nov) and what it changed here
- LiteLLM router ([request lifecycle](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/router/learn/request_lifecycle)): the router is the only
  retry loop, the provider call has retries off, and each retry may land on a different deployment. We had two retry
  loops (OmniRoute combo + llm.py asking the same combo again after a timeout). F1 follows their rule: per-model deadline
  inside the gateway, no client retry of a combo that timed out.
- [nvidia-litellm-router](https://github.com/rohansx/nvidia-litellm-router): NVIDIA's free NIM models are ~40 requests a
  minute each; it routes by measured latency. Our planner sends 8-14 calls in a row to one nemotron model and learn-deep
  shared it (F2). Next step if planning is still slow: spread plan calls over 2-3 NIM models by latency.
- [Gaming-Clipper](https://github.com/HmdNvd/Gaming-Clipper): picks moments from RMS audio-energy peaks plus the transcript,
  and uses a blurred-background "gaming-split" layout that keeps the HUD. Our clipper needs speech, so SEGA trailer
  campaigns give 0 clips ("almost no speech in asset 1-6", 20 Nov 17:35). Open item C1: an energy-peak mode for
  trailer / gameplay sources with a hook title and no captions.
- [MoneyPrinterTurbo](https://github.com/harry0703/MoneyPrinterTurbo) (the most-starred faceless pipeline): five linear
  stages (script, search terms, TTS, footage, ffmpeg) and no post-render judge. Its speed comes from few stages; our
  quality bar needs checks, so the gain is from running them side by side (F7) and judging once (F8), not adding more.
Content was rephrased for compliance with licensing restrictions.
