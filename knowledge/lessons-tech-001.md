# Lessons from tech-001 (external editor review, 16 Nov 2026: 5.0/10, "animated presentation, not a Reel")

The review was made on a re-encoded 512x910 copy (-16.2 LUFS); the master is 1080x1920, -13.1 LUFS.
Its editing points still hold. These are rules for every Studio video from now on.

1. Frame 0 says WHAT and WHY FOR ME. Start with the viewer's real task already happening
   ("₹20k ka phone, achha camera, 2 din battery" typed and answered), plus a 4-6 word promise
   ("GOOGLE SEARCH JUST GOT SMARTER"). A topic label ("SEARCH") is not a hook.
2. Proof is quick: 1-2 s per source shot, cropped to the ONE line and enlarged. Never hold an article for 9 s.
3. Every section gets a short on-screen headline (5-7 words) so it works with sound off. On-screen text is
   written separately from the captions; one primary text element at a time.
4. Show the real thing. Real product UI / screen captures beat drawn placeholders (coloured rectangles as
   phones read as fake). Illustrative data must be real and sourced, or visibly labelled "example".
5. No intentional "defects": typos with red squiggles read as an editing mistake, not a concept.
6. Each feature beat must say its benefit in plain words ("Ask in your language"), not make the viewer infer it.
7. Length follows information: ~30 s for this kind of update. Cut anything that adds no new information.
8. Ending 1.5-2 s: a question the viewer can answer in comments ("Kaunsa feature pehle try karoge?") + follow.
   No 7-8 s return to the opening graphic.
9. One visual identity per channel: one base, one accent, one secondary. Five full-screen ground colours in
   one video felt disconnected.
10. Keep one carrier (the search bar) morphing through every section; J-cut narration slightly ahead of visuals.
11. Do not answer feedback by adding effects. Fix the editorial decisions first.
