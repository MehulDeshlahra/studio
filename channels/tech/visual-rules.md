# Real visuals in tech reels (watched 14 on Instagram: big creators + hashtag pages, 2026-11-18)
High-performing tech reels prioritize physical interaction with devices over digital assets, using logos only as brief rhythmic markers. Relatability is achieved by showing tech through the lens of everyday habits, like scrolling common apps or reacting to real-time notifications.
Typical: typical counts: logos 1-3, app screens 5-8 (in reviews) or 12+ (in tutorials), product photos 2-4 out of 15-20 scenes

- The 'Reveal Rule': Never show the official brand logo in the first 5 seconds; introduce the product physically first and save the logo for the 'price' or 'specs' reveal (weighted by 300k+ like reels).
- Physicality Over Pixels: Always show app interfaces on a physical device being held by the creator rather than using static digital screenshots or mockups.
- The 3-Second Punctuation: Brand logos must only appear as rapid-fire visual punctuation (1.5–3s) exactly when the name is spoken, never lingering as a static background.
- Contextual UI: When demonstrating software, use 'relatable' apps (Netflix, WhatsApp, Messages) to show the screen in a real-world use case rather than empty system menus.
- The 'Search' Trust Factor: For app-based tutorials, always include a 2-second screen recording of the Play Store/App Store search process to prove the app is real and accessible.
- Metaphorical Branding: In 'Brand War' or 'Trolling' videos, use visual metaphors (e.g., a literal green apple) instead of official logos to increase shareability and bypass 'corporate' feel.
- Comparison Card System: For data-heavy comparisons (calories, price, speed), use small brand icons on high-contrast, stacked motion-graphic cards rather than full-screen logos.
- The 'Human Touch' Requirement: Every product shot or screen recording must feature a human element (a thumb scrolling, a hand holding the phone) to maintain a 'friend-to-friend' vibe.
- Logo Ceiling: Limit official brand logos to a maximum of 4 unique appearances per 60-second reel to avoid looking like a sponsored advertisement.
- Notification Realism: Use native OS elements (like an Android notification shade or iOS Control Center) to show 'success' states, making the tech feel integrated into the viewer's life.
