# jain bible: "Navkar Bhav" (@navkarbhav, added 18 Nov)
- Promise: one Jain story or practice in under 75 seconds, told simply and with respect, with the lesson it carries today.
- Audience: Jain families and young Jains across India and abroad, plus anyone curious about ahimsa and Jain ideas.
- Sources: Kalpasutra, Agam sutras (Acharanga, Uttaradhyayana, Gyatadharmakatha), Adipurana, Trishashti-shalaka-
  purusha-charitra (Hemachandra), Parishishtaparvan. Every reel names its source at the end ("Srot: Kalpasutra").
  When Shwetambar and Digambar versions differ, say so in one line, never pick a side.
- Pillars: (1) Tirthankara kathayein (Rishabhdev to Mahavir Swami) (2) saints, kings and devotees (Chandanbala,
  Bahubali, Shrenik, Meghkumar, Sthulibhadra) (3) why Jains do it: Paryushan, Samvatsari, no food after sunset,
  Navkar Mantra, Micchami Dukkadam, explained simply (4) festival reels on the day (Mahavir Jayanti, Paryushan,
  Diwali = Mahavir Nirvan, Kartik Purnima).
- Look: serene painterly style, saffron, gold and white marble, calm. Tirthankaras ONLY as serene meditating temple
  idols (padmasana or kayotsarga, eyes half closed, with their emblem) or as symbols (lotus, light, footprints);
  never as action characters, never with weapons. Monks and nuns in plain white with a rajoharan. Never Hindu gods.
- Voice: calm, gentle Hindi storyteller, soft and warm, clear Prakrit/Sanskrit names.
- Structure: 1 s hook (the surprising moment or question) -> the story in 6-10 beats -> the lesson in one line ->
  a question to the viewer ("Aap hote toh kya karte?") -> "Jai Jinendra".
- Rules: respectful, no sect or religion bashing, no politics, no miracles claimed as history, no medical claims.
- Never: on-screen AI label.
